About

I'm a third-year Ph.D. student in Physics at the University of New Hampshire, working in Dr. Jiadong Zang's group. My research is focused on developing a microscopic understanding of magnetic materials using quantum mechanics, many body physics and AI, specifically for strongly correlated materials and transition metal oxides. My aim is to contribute to the creation of cheaper and more efficent permanent and soft magnets.

My day-to-day work primarily involves me writing simulation code from scratch, and implementing AI/ML-based methods to understand or screen magnetic materials.

I also do work with Dr. Shawna Hollen studying 2D materials. My work studies the relationship between electronic properties and quasiparticle scattering.

Research

Current · Zang Group

Rare-Earth Free Permanent Magnets

I'm building computational models from scratch to understand the microscopic mechanisms behind magnetic crystaline anisotropy in strongly correlated transition metal oxides. My results can be used to design better rare-earth free permanent magnets and soft magnets.
  • Correlated magnetism
  • Transition metal oxides
  • Materials design

Current · Zang Group

AI/ML For Materials Discovery

I work with my group develop to AI based pipelines for materials discovery. This often involves replacing traditional expensive computational methods with machine learning models to accelerate how quickly we can calculate our materials.
  • AI/ML
  • Materials discovery
  • Computational modeling

2D materials · Hollen Lab

Phase Singularities in Real Materials

I'm finishing a project studying how phase singularities can be used to reveal properties of wave media and scatterers. I'm using scattering in graphene as my playground, but this work has applications to classical wave systems across different energy scales.
  • Phase singularities
  • 2D materials
  • Wave propagation
